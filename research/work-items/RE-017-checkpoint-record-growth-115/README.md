# RE-017 · Checkpoint record growth limits

Target Linux x86-64 Node #115 SHA-256 a6e2897cd051213d004802deca416e8eca62b7a8278fa39f249e98c3867b5301. The [checkpoint entry packet](../RE-017-entry-checkpoint-115/README.md) identifies the two row pipelines. This packet follows their growth calls at 0x153fb25 and 0x1540c51 into 0x14cac50 and 0x14cab60.

The callers pass an additional count of one, alignment input eight, and a stride of 32 or 24 bytes respectively. The wrapper checks carry on logical length plus additional count. On the ordinary path it takes the maximum of that sum, twice the old capacity with word-sized arithmetic, and a stride-dependent floor. The floor is eight for stride one, four for strides two through 1024, and one for larger strides. Both selected record callers therefore use four. The nested helper checks the full-width unsigned product of chosen capacity and stride, then compares its low word to 2^63 minus the alignment input. For the selected callers the permitted upper bound is 2^63−8 bytes, inclusive.

Given alignment eight, a nonzero old capacity uses the resize slot with the old pointer and new byte count. A zero old capacity and nonzero byte count use the fresh allocation slot. An allocation returning a null pointer produces tag one and the alignment and size words. A rejected size produces tag one with zero at offset eight. The wrapper forwards the two error words to the same failure slot. On success it publishes the returned pointer and selected capacity to the descriptor at offsets eight and zero. The caller's logical length is outside these stores. The other alignment branches and allocator side effects are outside the selected record contract.

It checks the pinned ELF, both unwind extents, selected operand windows, transfers and five relocations. Eighteen synthetic full-buffer requests cover the floor, byte bound and count wrap for both record strides.
